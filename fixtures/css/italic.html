The lighthouse keeper's almanac

Reviewed by the reading circle, autumn meeting

A slim book of weather notes, kept for forty-one years on a rock off the northern cape. The keeper writes of fog that arrives like a guest and leaves without a word, of gulls, of the supply boat, and of the long winter when the lamp's clockwork failed and was wound by hand every hour.

"The sea was flat as a plate this morning, and I could not trust it."

Readers who prefer a plot will be disappointed; readers who like lists of wind directions will not. The final chapter, A Year Without Storms, is the strongest.
