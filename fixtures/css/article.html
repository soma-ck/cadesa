What a week of measuring line breaks taught us

Noor Haddad · 9 min read

Text that wraps one word earlier than you expected moves everything under it. We spent a week finding out why.

Most layout bugs that reach production are not exotic. A heading wraps at one width and not another, a button label grows by a word after translation, a card gets one line taller than its neighbours.

Measure first

Before changing any style, we measured every text box on the page at every width from phones to wide desktops, and kept the numbers next to the source.

The surprises were all in the margins: the browser's own spacing around headings and paragraphs, and the way two margins meet and become one.

What we found

  • Headings wrap more often than body text.
  • Margins between paragraphs collapse into one.
Measure the page you ship, not the page you designed.

Every number in this post came from the browser itself.

We compared each box at every ten pixels of width.
